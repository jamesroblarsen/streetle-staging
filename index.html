          How to play

          Each day there's a new city, shown as an unlabelled 3D model: a small piece of it, chosen to show how the city was planned or how it grew. Drag with two fingers (or the right mouse button) to tilt and turn it.

          You have six guesses. After each wrong guess the model gives a little more away:

          1. The street plan and buildings at their real heights, 800 m across
          2. A wider view, 1 km across
          3. 1.3 km, with the places where people drink coloured on the model
          4. 2 km, with places of worship by faith
          5. Zoomed right out to 4 km, the model in the middle of the streets around it, with a kind of place picked for that city and a few local street names
          6. 10 km: the whole layout of the city, its rivers, coast and roads, plus the country, when the city was founded and how it ranks there by size: pick one of its cities

          The first kind of place is always where people drink (pubs and bars against cafés), the second is always places of worship by faith, and the third is whichever kind of place says most about that city: sports grounds, bazaars, eating out, embassies, old walls, shops and more. Tap a legend to choose which kind is coloured.

          For your first four guesses you only learn whether your city shares the answer's hemisphere, continent, side of the road, main faith and street-sign script. Distance and direction appear from guess 5, but a guess that matches all five gets the direction arrow straight away, and two in a row get the distance too.

          Not sure of the name? Type a country to see all of its cities, biggest first.

          Look at the grain of the streets: grids and their angles, ring roads where walls stood, medieval tangles, diagonals cut through blocks, courtyards, towers, block sizes.
